# Snäx Swiss vending product shortlist

## Current recommendation — health-first revision, 14 September 2026

**This revision replaces the original 25-product recommendation and 14-SKU pilot below.** Prioritize low sugar, simple ingredients and vending suitability ahead of brand popularity or expected sales volume. The revised choice set contains **8 candidates: 6 pass the desk-based nutrition/ingredient screen and 2 remain conditional**. Start with the six screened choices; add the two conditional nut products only after their missing label information is resolved. No energy drinks are included.

The original research is retained in the collapsed archive for sourcing and decision traceability. Its “Core,” “Trial,” pricing and pilot recommendations are historical, not current purchasing instructions. This is a research update; the website's four-product editorial selection has not been changed. In particular, its Rivella Rot and protein wafer no longer fit this recommendation; Alpahirt remains unsuitable for automatic ambient approval.

### Selection rules

These are **Snäx purchasing preferences**, not legal nutrition-claim definitions or a medical classification of foods:

- **Drinks:** target 0 g labelled sugar; maximum 0.5 g/100 ml and 2.5 g per sale unit. No added sugar, syrup, honey or juice concentrate used for sweetness; no sweeteners. Exclude energy drinks, including sugar-free versions.
- **Snacks:** maximum 5 g labelled sugar/100 g **and** 5 g per entire sale unit. Prefer nuts, seeds, pulses and wholegrain crackers. A small sugary bar does not pass merely because its portion is small. Dried fruit and date bars are not automatic replacements.
- **Ingredients:** exclude artificial flavourings and sweetener-based confectionery, including polyol-sweetened bars. An unspecified “Aroma” is **unverified**, not proof of an artificial flavour. Hold such products until clarified. Explicitly natural aromas are acceptable in an optional drink; plain water remains the default. This is a preference for the assortment, not a claim that permitted additives are inherently unsafe.
- **Overall fit:** low sugar alone is insufficient. Do not promote processed meat or ordinary fried chips as healthy staples. Prefer unsalted nuts; review salt, saturated fat and energy per full pack. The retained 50 g nut bags provide substantial energy, so do not describe them as low-calorie portions.
- **Operations:** factory-sealed, individually saleable packs only, with ambient storage confirmed for the actual machine temperature. No bulk repacking, refrigerated replacements or unverified long-life claims.

### Starting pilot and tradeoffs

Start with **H1–H6: three drinks, one nut mix and two wholegrain crackers**. Add H7 and H8 only when their conditional checks pass; prefer H7 over additional salted-nut capacity. This deliberately reduces the former 14-SKU pilot rather than filling slots with disqualified products. Give water and the proven snack options extra lanes if the machine has more slots. There is no requirement to reach 25 distinct products.

No current six-product pilot item replaces a 20–26 g protein bar nutritionally. Nuts and crackers offer smaller protein contributions. H8 may sit in the Protein merchandising category once its values are confirmed, but do not claim that it is equivalent to a protein supplement. Do not force category coverage by relabelling an unsuitable product.

Sales volume and margin for replacements are unknown; accept that tradeoff explicitly. The M-Budget energy sales evidence is historical demand context only and no longer supports an inclusion recommendation. Measure units per available day, contribution after refill/delivery costs, stockouts and expiry waste over four to six weeks. Select winners **within the health screen**; do not reintroduce sugary products simply because they sell faster.

Plain roasted chickpeas or edamame remain future protein leads, not counted candidates: the Swiss KoRo listings found were 1 kg and 750 g bulk packs, respectively, not suitable single-vend portions.[^48] No repacking is proposed. Qualify a factory-sealed small pack if more protein variety is needed. Likewise, unsweetened tea must have a verified Swiss SKU, ingredients and ambient specification before being added.

Retain the original operating checks: log machine temperature, obtain written storage limits and minimum remaining life, validate individual-sale labelling/allergens, test dispensing and plan replenishment from actual lane capacity. A “120-day” statement does not establish 120 days remaining at delivery.

## Recommendation

This draft proposes **25 products: 6 Protein, 10 Getränk, 3 Low Carb, and 6 Snack**. It combines established Swiss retail products with fitness snacks and small packs suitable for vending. The operating assumption is an **indoor, unrefrigerated machine**. Research was checked on **14 September 2026**; this is a selection draft for review, not an approved purchase order.

Start with water, a small selection of regular and reduced-sugar drinks, crackers, portioned nuts, and a few protein products. Chocolate-coated products need a temperature check before inclusion. Kägi recommends 16–18°C for optimal quality: an uncooled machine in a warm room does not necessarily provide suitable storage.[^1]

There is no defensible public ranking of the 25 bestselling individual products across Migros, Coop, and Switzerland. The strongest specific sales evidence found is **M-Budget Energy Drink: 1,966,051 units and tenth place among Migros Aare supermarket products in 2025**. This is regional retailer evidence, not a national ranking. Most other candidates have evidence of distribution, established brand demand, or customer reviews rather than disclosed unit sales.[^2]

Long shelf life and infrequent refilling are separate requirements. A product can remain within its best-before date for months and still sell out in days. The assortment should therefore be approved together with minimum remaining shelf life at delivery, pack dimensions, and a realistic refill interval.

## How to read the shortlist

Each product has one primary category, matching the existing Snäx categories. A protein drink would belong primarily to Protein; ordinary drinks remain Getränk, including zero-sugar variants. Low Carb is a merchandising grouping here, not a certification or a claim that a product causes fat loss.

**Demand evidence:**

- **Sales:** published quantity or ranking, with its geographical and time limits.
- **Brand:** manufacturer or retailer evidence about the brand/range; not proof that the selected flavour and pack is a bestseller.
- **Retail:** a Swiss retail listing or customer feedback; no verified sales rank.
- **Supply:** a manufacturer or trade listing that establishes a sourcing route; no popularity claim.

**Decision:** “Core” means a strong first-pilot candidate, “Trial” means test a small quantity, and “Reserve” means resolve a specific issue before allocating a slot. Every candidate still requires a delivery-date and packaging check. These are recommendations, not sales forecasts.

**Shelf-life notation:**

- **30d receipt:** the cited Coop page explicitly guarantees at least 30 days after receipt. This is a delivery minimum, not the product’s full shelf life, and is too short to underwrite a multi-month unattended stock plan.
- **120d stated:** the manufacturer lists 120 days minimum durability. Confirm whether this runs from manufacture, dispatch, or receipt.
- **Not disclosed:** no reliable duration was established for the selected pack. Obtain the actual batch date and a written minimum remaining life.

Prices are public CHF reference prices observed on the cited pages, sometimes through indexed content. They exclude delivery and are **not wholesale quotes or proposed vending prices**. Multipack divisions are explicitly shown. Stock and prices may differ by location and change before ordering. A product page marked unavailable remains evidence of a listing, not evidence of current supply.

## Shelf life and remote operation

### Procurement conditions

For an initial request for quotation, propose **at least 90 days remaining at receipt**, with **120–180 days preferred** for slower-selling products. These are Snäx purchasing targets, not verified lifetimes of the listed products. A supplier may be unable to meet them for a particular SKU; that should change the purchasing quantity or selection.

Ask each supplier to confirm:

1. Exact product name, flavour, GTIN, net contents and case quantity.
2. Total unopened shelf life and guaranteed remaining life at delivery, separately.
3. Minimum/maximum storage temperature, humidity/light requirements and permitted transport excursions.
4. Actual batch best-before date and lot before dispatch.
5. Individual pack dimensions, packaging type and saleable-unit labelling.
6. Delivered case price, delivery charge, minimum order, reorder lead time and substitution policy.

For the products with only Coop’s 30-day receipt guarantee, buying online without further agreement does not meet the proposed 90-day target. A longer date printed on a sample proves only that batch, not subsequent supply.

### Operating assumptions

Use a shaded indoor location and log the temperature **inside the machine**, including a warm period. Room temperature varies with season, sun exposure and heat from the equipment. A thermostat-free room that reaches 28°C should not be treated as equivalent to Kägi’s 16–18°C quality recommendation.[^1]

Keep only factory-sealed units. Do not buy bulk nuts and rebag them for this pilot: that introduces packaging, hygiene, date and labelling work. Load the earliest-expiring stock first and retain the batch/date against each refill.

Schedule visits using both stock consumption and remaining life. As an illustration, if a lane holds 12 items and sells one per day, it has 12 days of stock even if the product has six months remaining. Remote stock reporting helps reduce unnecessary journeys; a person still needs to refill, clean, inspect and resolve failed vends.

For a proposed 28-day visit interval, a lane selling one item per day needs at least 28 units plus a chosen buffer. If that exceeds its capacity, use more lanes, shorten the interval or arrange a local replenishment partner. Test sealed packs in the actual mechanism before ordering cases: slim BiFi sticks, broad chip bags and fragile crackers have different dispensing risks.

## Suggested first pilot

The 25-product draft is a choice set, not a recommendation to give every product equal space. A practical starting selection is **14 SKUs**:

- **Protein:** P1 Chiefs Crispy Cookie, P2 Barebells Caramel Choco, P3 PowerBar Chocolate Nut.
- **Getränk:** D1 Valser Still, D2 Coca-Cola Zero, D4 Rivella Rot, D6 Kult Lemon, D7 Kult Lemon Zero.
- **Low Carb:** L1 QUIX peanuts, L2 Naturaplan nut mix.
- **Snack:** S1 DAR-VIDA, S2 Blévita, S3 Farmer Apple, S4 Zweifel Paprika.

This balances five drink choices, three protein bars and six other snacks. The protein-bar selection is conditional on temperature suitability. If that fails, retain the cooler-independent categories and qualify another protein format rather than assume the coating will survive.

After a four-to-six-week pilot, compare units sold per available day, stockout days, gross contribution after delivery/refill costs, failed vends and units nearing expiry. Avoid ranking products only by total units if they had different availability. Give the winners more capacity before adding more flavours.

Public retail prices suggest potentially expensive starting costs for Chiefs chips, jerky and premium bars. They do not establish a profitable vending price. Calculate contribution from actual delivered purchase cost, payment fees, spoilage and allocated refill travel. A CHF 0.40 supermarket energy drink also needs thoughtful positioning: a large vending markup may be conspicuous even when the absolute convenience price is modest.

## Implications for later onboarding and weekly snack plans

The four merchandising categories are not enough to generate a plan. For each **approved, physically stocked SKU**, retain nutrition per entire sale unit, full ingredients and allergens, dietary suitability, caffeine where known, pack size, verified source/date and current availability. Keep product identity separate from flavour substitutions; a replacement bar can change allergens and protein content.

The planner should describe a **weekly snack contribution**, not a complete weekly diet. Height, weight and a goal alone do not describe someone’s total food intake or training. Portion energy, preferences, dietary exclusions and the rest of their diet matter. Swiss nutrition guidance treats balanced eating as the foundation and does not make protein supplements a requirement for recreational sport.[^40]

A useful explanation would be “this pack adds approximately 20 g protein,” with a choice of alternatives. Avoid claims that a particular snack burns fat or builds muscle by itself. Ordinary sweet drinks can remain available commercially while water and appropriate lower-sugar alternatives receive different planning priority.

No onboarding, recommendation engine or weekly plan has been implemented as part of this research draft.
